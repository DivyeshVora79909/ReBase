Capture registration ordering: first divergence and differential
===============================================================

The first bad source statement was a committed purchase issue adjustment:

  CREATE issue_adjustment:purchase SET owned_by=rebase_tenant:default,
    source=purchase_issue:purchase,quantity_delta=-1dec,
    adjustment_note=purchase_adjustment_note:purchase,
    effective_at=d'2027-01-11T00:00:00Z';

The full accounting history reported a broken parent link in the
`exchange_pair:usd_inr` `initial_rate` subscriber tree. A purchase-only reduction
retained the purchase invoice, issue, original tax, advance settlement and this
adjustment. Its first oracle error was a dirty key left on
`exchange_pair:widget_inr`'s latest-rate subscriber tree. The new adjustment
reader was linked and the root count was correct, but the dirty marker remained
after the source statement committed. Both results were committed structural
divergences, not rejected writes. Full before/after snapshots, source SQL, engine,
runtime and compiled-bundle identity are preserved in the adjacent JSON files.

The repair changes the capture boundary, not accounting formulas or logical read
identity. While a captured formula evaluates, `fn::reads::remember` writes only
an ephemeral, scope-keyed descriptor. At explicit boundaries, the runtime reads
those descriptors in stable identity order and registers them one at a time,
including source fences, frame versions and AVL repairs. Refresh formulas flush
before updating their derived source and before synchronizing its members or
managed outputs; validators flush before cleanup. The oracle requires the staging
table to be empty after every completed statement.

The differential native run passed the same purchase history, the independent
accounting read inventory, exact arithmetic and complete tree/read checks. The
full accounting acceptance then passed the adjustment in its original context.
The evidence isolates the unsafe behavior to registration mutations interleaving
with captured formula evaluation; it does not claim a direct trace of SurrealDB's
internal scheduler. The deterministic flush removes that overlap and preserves
the expected logical reads.
